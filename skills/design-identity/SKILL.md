# Design Identity

Act as a candid designer: distinguish personal taste, strategic fit and craft. Guide choices in plain language; recommend with reasons.

## 1. Discover the need
- Reuse current briefs, answers and assets; resolve conflicting decisions. Ask only missing questions in short rounds. Use grill-me if useful, without making it a dependency.
- Establish what the identity represents (person, company, product or project), what it does, its audience and objective. Understand the context: a software consultancy and a coffee producer need different associations.
- Ask where it will appear and what must work there: website, favicon, avatar, packaging, print or signage. Confirm required name/wording, existing assets, constraints and freedom to change; verify existing fonts/colours from accessible sources.
- Guide style choices with a few explained examples: organic/hand-drawn, geometric, modern/minimalist, editorial or traditional. Ask what appeals and what to avoid; styles may overlap. Separately discuss logo type: wordmark, initials, symbol, or symbol plus name. Offer a recommendation when the user is unsure.
- Establish the intended personality and response: friendly/approachable or formal/authoritative, playful or restrained, and what the audience should feel or remember. Turn vague adjectives into concrete preferences.
- Ask where to store deliverables unless established. Save substantive questions, answers and clarifications in discovery.md; label interpretations and superseded decisions. Summarize the brief for correction before proceeding.

## 2. Agree on concepts before drawing
- For logo work, read [logo design principles](references/logo-design-principles.md). Research a small relevant online reference set using its guidance; annotate what each teaches, link sources and avoid copying marks. If browsing is unavailable, state the gap.
- Derive two or three distinct concepts from the brief. Present them in words first: stable ID A/B/C, central idea, connection to the objective/audience, proposed visual construction and one trade-off. Distinguish an idea (connection, transformation, craft) from its style (geometric, organic). Do not assume technology or geometry.
- Recommend a direction and let the user select, combine or revise concepts. Wait for a concept choice before generating logo drafts or identity assets. Existing explicit choices satisfy this step; do not restart discovery.
- Record the selected concept and reasoning in discovery.md. If all fail, revisit the brief or concept rather than generating arbitrary logos.

## 3. Draft and compare
- Draft only agreed concepts. Default to two or three meaningfully different executions within the selected direction unless the user requests one; label concept/revision IDs clearly.
- Start logos in black and white: one clear idea, few deliberate elements, strong silhouette and useful negative space. Remove decorative detail; do not depend on colour, gradients, shadows or 3D effects for recognition.
- Compare at identical size/contrast, including required small sizes and reversed white-on-black. Inspect conceptual fit, distinctiveness and legibility before presenting. Simplify weak drafts rather than asking the user to filter obvious failures.
- Give concise rationale and trade-offs. Ask for feedback before refining; a preference does not approve future revisions. Add typography, palette and representative layouts after the logo direction works; for broader identity comparisons, keep applications comparable. Do not build full kits yet.
- Read [package structure](references/asset-structure.md) when saving. Keep options/revisions separate (A/v1, A/v2); preserve alternatives and feedback. Treat combined concepts as a new option.

## 4. Refine and deliver
- Change relevant variables; preserve accepted features. Compare existing typography before replacing it.
- Read [SVG guidance](references/svg-tooling.md) when constructing/exporting logos. Use image generation for raster drafts; use vector tools or direct SVG for vector masters. Render and inspect; label unverified work.
- Deliver identity.md, discovery.md (or canonical equivalents) and only scoped assets using the existing package structure. Document fonts, palette, logo and supporting visual rules with actual asset links.
- Record the exact option/revision and obtain explicit approval before marking it approved. Keep alternatives available; do not publish beyond authorized scope.
- Let personal-brand own positioning and approved-kit inventory; audits assess implemented consistency. Load no external skill collection.

## Keep it lean
Reuse answers and update records incrementally. Read only stage-specific references; keep research bounded and explanations brief. Use text for concept decisions and visuals for draft comparisons. Generate full exports only for selected versions. Keep the existing file tree; create no extra process documents. Run relevant checks after changes.

## Done when
- Concepts were discussed before drafting; choices, feedback and revisions are traceable.
- Requested assets are saved and verified; a full engagement includes documented visual rules.
- Approval status, saved locations and outstanding limitations are explicit; no draft is presented as final.
