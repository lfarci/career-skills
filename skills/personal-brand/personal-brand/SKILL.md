# Personal Brand

Define the selected public professional narrative and reusable wording for different audiences. Treat private profile facts, north star, interests, action plans and internal decisions as inputs, not permission to publish. Exclude private doubts, constraints and plans unless explicitly selected for public use; label aspirations without implying achievements. Do not rewrite source facts to match a preferred slogan.

## Workspace
Read the chosen career workspace index and relevant canonical records when available. Follow its source map and privacy/write boundaries; link evidence and outputs instead of duplicating them. Without an index, use supplied inputs and ask only task-relevant gaps. Work with Markdown wherever stored; do not require setup, a provider or folder migration.

## Steps

1. Review verified professional history, user's intended career direction and constraints. Separate existing strengths from aspirational capabilities.
2. Identify important audiences (recruiters, hiring managers, technical peers, collaborators), public surfaces and any **existing visual brand assets** (banners, logos, avatars/icons, color palette and fonts). Reuse what exists before suggesting new designs.
3. Draft a concise core positioning and evidence-backed differentiators. Explain any proposed change to established messaging; surface competing positioning choices for human judgment.
4. Maintain a canonical public messaging document (or existing equivalent) with reusable short/long bios, headlines, introductions and project descriptions as scoped. Link surface-specific adaptations to their source and intended audience; do not duplicate private records or invent claims.
5. Define the distinct purpose of each surface. Different headlines and appropriately sized or cropped graphics can communicate one consistent identity.
6. When visual consistency matters, maintain or propose a **small optional brand kit** that points to reusable approved banners, logos, icons, palette colors and fonts. Read [the lightweight brand-kit guide](references/brand-kit.md) only for this branch. Recommend or create new assets only when needed and requested; avoid mandatory design systems.
   When original visual identity design is requested, use `define-design-brief` for discovery, `create-design-assets` for concepts/production, or `review-design` for critique when available; keep positioning here. Remain independently useful without it.
7. Determine which assets merit an individual audit or a full cross-asset alignment audit. Use `audit-surface` and `audit` when available, but remain independently functional if they are not installed.
8. Produce prioritized recommendations or reviewable proposed edits. Never claim a platform has changed until the modification is confirmed, and ask before public publishing.

## Output: Professional Brand Plan

- Core identity and positioning choices
- Intended audiences and evidence-backed differentiators
- Surface inventory and individual purpose
- Canonical reusable public text snippets and surface-specific adaptations
- Content/message guidance for each relevant surface
- Optional visual brand-kit links and usage guidance when applicable
- Prioritized actions, dependencies and approval needs

## Done when

- Positioning is coherent with verified career facts and selected goals.
- Legitimate wording and platform-specific visual variants are not misidentified as inconsistency.
- When visual identity is in scope, approved assets and design tokens are reused instead of needlessly recreated.
- Each recommendation names a target surface and expected outcome.
- Plans/experiments are not presented as accomplished production work.
- No unauthorized public changes occur.
