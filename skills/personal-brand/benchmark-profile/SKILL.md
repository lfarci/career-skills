# Benchmark Profile

## Frame the comparison
- Read the user's selected profile/resume and target roles, audience, experience level, domain and geography when relevant. Ask only missing information that materially changes comparator selection. Agree on the surfaces and depth; do not require a complete career interview.
- Separate comparable peers from aspirational examples. Explain relevance and important differences; do not treat a famous profile, prestigious employer or senior title as proof of effective presentation.
- Use only necessary professional information. Never submit private resumes, unpublished goals or client-sensitive details to external search services; search with generic role/domain criteria unless public identifiers are needed and authorized.

## Research public examples
- Search the web for a small relevant set of profiles, portfolios and publicly shared resumes. Prefer the person's own website or original resume over reposts and template galleries. Verify and inspect each source before using it; include URLs and checked dates.
- Distinguish real career examples from fictional/template resumes. Note seniority, market and career-path differences that limit comparison. Respect access boundaries; do not bypass sign-in or assume inaccessible LinkedIn profiles were inspected.
- If research is unavailable, compare only supplied examples and state the narrower coverage. Never invent examples, links, qualifications or hiring outcomes. Keep research bounded; stop once additional examples no longer change the recommendations.

## Compare and recommend
- Compare positioning, specificity of evidence, project/achievement descriptions, structure, readability, portfolio navigation and visual presentation as relevant. Judge the user's goals and intended audience, not superficial similarity or popularity.
- Return a concise comparison table: source, peer/aspirational/template status, why relevant, useful pattern and limitations. Cite the source for each observation; distinguish observations from your interpretation.
- Prioritize a few improvements tied to specific user assets. Separate communication improvements using existing evidence from experience/skill gaps that need future development. A peer's credential or metric is not something to add without evidence; no unsupported numeric resume scores.
- Adapt principles rather than copy distinctive wording, designs or personal claims. A useful pattern may be a clear project outcome, not that person's exact achievement or style. Preserve the user's voice and legitimate differences.

## Hand off
- Ask where to save findings unless established; reuse existing records and link canonical assets. Provide findings in the response if saving is unavailable; do not claim a saved report or changed profile without confirmation.
- Offer selected recommendations to personal-brand for reusable messaging, build-resume for resume changes, or the design skills for visual work when available. Refer genuine growth opportunities to the private career-direction records. Each handoff must remain useful without another installed skill.
- Do not edit, publish, contact comparison subjects or apply for jobs unless separately requested.

## Done when
- Inspected examples are linked and their relevance and limits are explicit.
- Recommendations identify concrete improvements and separate presentation from future development.
- Research gaps, source dates and uninspected surfaces are clear; no copying or invented outcomes occur.
